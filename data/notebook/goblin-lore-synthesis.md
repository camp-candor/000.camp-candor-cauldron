## 0. Executive summary

`goblin-lore` is not a wiki. It is an **offline, compile-time legislative authority** for a fictional universe: a strictly typed relational database authorable in Markdown, which rejects invalid fictional states *before* any expensive generation (language models, TTS, GPU rendering) begins.

The single most valuable idea, stated in the source and sharpened in review:

> **The narrative world has laws; the simulation explores consequences of those laws; media is a projection of the resulting state. Generative models are subordinate rendering workers with no authority over the world.**

Everything else follows from keeping five things distinct and giving every transition between them an explicit, testable contract:

1. what the world **permits** (canon),
2. what **happened** (event history),
3. what is **currently true** (materialized state, *derived* from 1 and 2),
4. what a character **believes**, and
5. what **artifact** was actually produced.

The review's central lesson about the *document itself*:

> **A specification becomes trustworthy exactly where claims have been converted into derivable properties, executable tests, and explicit boundaries. Its remaining weaknesses are almost all places where it asserts a rule without specifying the mechanism that makes the rule true.**

## 1. The diegetic universe (the fiction the system serves) **[SOURCE]**

The architecture is not decoration around an arbitrary story. The fiction and the engineering reinforce each other, which is why the design feels coherent.

**Core thesis of the story.** A family so terrified of being seen by the human world that they have become experts at never truly seeing *each other*. Every domestic interaction, duel, leverage exchange, and silence is an inward reflection of hiding.

**Geography as drama.** The family lives in crawlspaces beneath a human house. Space is not backdrop; it is contested territory:

- **The Warm Pipe**: the shared hub that ticks as it cools at night, a sound the family has collectively agreed not to fear.
- **The Pantry Wall**: Bog's contested territory, smelling of coveted, resented human food.
- **The Attic Stair**: the coldest, most exposed threshold, carrying human acoustics with painful clarity.
- **The Splinter Gap**: a transitional crawlway linking territories.

**Hierarchy of fear.** (1) *The Upstairs*, the humans as ambient environmental pressure rather than individuals; (2) *Discovery mid-act*, being caught not merely existing but revealing vulnerability; (3) *The Thing Nobody Names*, the historical trauma referenced only as `the_famine`.

**Cast.**

| Character | Function |
|---|---|
| **Bog** | Older, controlling; remembers `the_famine`; obsessed with status and bean-stack tallies; maintains an unspoken master–servant dynamic (e.g., over scouring the good silver spoon). |
| **Spleen** | Independent, guarded, non-aligned; clutches unfinished wooden carvings; refuses to be intimidated. |
| **Wart** | Youngest; no memory of the famine; guilt-driven, over-explains to pre-empt accusation. Mechanically a *Guardian* (Spite 1, Warmth 3) with ability *Shield Ally*. |
| **Mum Grissel** | Elder with passive authority; arguments halt mid-sentence when she enters the pantry. |

**Economy of scraps.** Currency is physical and weight-classed: *Scrap* (common: nails, thread), *Shine* (uncommon: buttons, beads, foil, used as social currency for debts), *Weight* (rare: tools, load-bearing items, preserved rations). Because economics is embodied, a social debt can be dramatized as a literal object.

**Deliberately unresolved canon.** The universe mandates open questions that generation engines are *barred from closing*: the true nature of `the_famine`; the fate of the family member who vanished before the timeline; what `the_pantry_watcher` is; whether other goblin societies exist. **Unknown is a legitimate, protected state.** This guards against a specific language-model failure: converting ambiguity into fabricated certainty.

**The correspondence that makes the design elegant.** *Diegetically*, the family lives inside a constrained, hidden system. *Architecturally*, the narrative engine lives inside a constrained system. *Dramatically*, characters cannot act outside their physical, social, and epistemic limits. *Computationally*, the generator cannot produce states outside compiled invariants.

## 2. System architecture as it exists in the source **[SOURCE]**

### 2.1 Repository shape

```text
goblin-lore/
├── characters/        # dossiers
├── grievances/        # structured social debts, escalation ceilings, outcome branches
├── locations/         # geography, sensory anchors, adjacency
├── possessions/       # tracked items, equipment slots, weight classes
├── world/             # mythology, economy, classes, event registry
├── schemas/           # Zod contracts
├── scripts/compile.ts # compiler, cycle detector, deterministic hasher
└── compiled/          # committed JSON (bible-state.json, characters.json)
```

**Corpus-first philosophy.** Static canon and runtime state are completely segregated. `goblin-lore` defines *what is true about the world*; runtime engines track dynamic variables (hex positions, paranoia clocks, somatic injuries) in a separate relational ledger.

### 2.2 The compiler: three passes, not two

The source calls it a "two-pass compiler" in several places (lines 86, 94, 124, 190, 268, 285, 410, 3497) but defines three passes at line 5651. **The accurate description is a three-pass compiler**: two validation passes plus a deterministic sealing pass.

| Pass | Role |
|---|---|
| **Pass 1: Shape & Invariants** | `gray-matter` + `zod` isolate YAML frontmatter and validate scalar ranges, slot counts, enum membership, and physical/somatic invariants. |
| **Pass 2: Relational Integrity & Causal DAG** | Sweeps the entity graph so every reference (equipped possession, grievance target, secret ID, spatial link) resolves; detects causal cycles. Any dangling pointer is a **Hard Fail** (`process.exit(1)`, no output written). |
| **Pass 3: Deterministic Seal** | Recursively sorts keys, normalizes, hashes with SHA-256, writes `compiled/bible-state.json` atomically. |

The "five-stage verification pipeline" (line 2601) similarly numbers **Stages 0 through 5, which is six stages.** Trivial, but a governance document should be internally exact.

### 2.3 The zero-drift tripwire

CI runs `scripts/compile.ts` then `git diff --exit-code compiled/`. If a human edits Markdown without committing the recompiled JSON, the build fails, preventing downstream engines from running on stale data. Rule 4 (Section 17): **no runtime Markdown parsing**; downstream consumers use only the compiled artifact.

### 2.4 Downstream execution

1. **The Sower** (`worker-sower-engine`): reads compiled canon read-only; runs PRNG-weighted lotteries (Stage 3) by proximity and tension to pair characters; resolves arguments with 2d6 outcome math against stats like Spite and Cunning (Stage 4); compiles a screenplay AST.
2. **Audio & assets**: local TTS synthesizes lines into integer frame counts; manifests bind emotions to sprite sheets.
3. **The Viewport** (`goblin-viewport-template`): stamps isolated episode folders with start frames, camera interpolation, viseme animation baked into static TypeScript, plus an immutable `render_manifest.json`.

### 2.5 Multi-series generalization **[SOURCE]**

The source proposes separating the **meta-ontology** (schemas, compiler) from **story instances** (`series/under-the-floorboards`, `deep-space-salvage`, `wiregrass-basin`), and formalizes a narrative universe as a tuple **⟨𝒮, 𝒜, ℐ, ℛₘ⟩**: state space, possible actions, invariants, medium-projection rules. Reduced: *a narrative universe is a state space constrained by laws, transformed by actions, and projected into a medium.*

## 3. The layered model of truth

### 3.1 The source's tripartite boundary **[SOURCE]**

**Event Log → Materialized State → Media Projection.** Media changes must never silently mutate world facts. Runtime state lives in a relational ledger, not in the Markdown.

### 3.2 The refined model **[REVIEW]**

The review sharpened this into **four primitives and one derived view**:

| Layer | Nature | Source of truth? | Rebuildable from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Canon** | What the world *permits*. Versioned; immutable **within a revision** (not literally immutable). | Yes | nothing |
| **Event log** | What *happened*. Append-only, linear, hash-chained. | Yes | nothing |
| **Recorded intermediates** | Approved dialogue ASTs, TTS waveforms, measured durations, recorded model outputs, seed states. | Yes | nothing |
| **Media artifacts** | Approved renders and audio masters. Evidence, not cache. | Yes (as evidence) | nothing (non-reproducible) |
| **Materialized state** | What is *currently true*. | **No: derived** | canon + events |

The governing equation:

$$\text{State}_N = \text{fold}(\text{apply},\ \text{State}_0,\ [E_1,\dots,E_N])$$

**Any materialized state must be regenerable from canon plus events. A build that cannot do this is broken.** Treating state as an independent source of truth would create a second authority that will eventually disagree with the first.

### 3.3 The Deletion Principle **[SOURCE, sharpened in REVIEW]**

> If all generative models are deleted, the universe remains logically intact in the ledger. If the ledger is lost, the universe suffers brain death even while the models keep producing pixels and dialogue.

The source uses it for lifecycle (retired entities move to `_retired/` rather than being deleted, preserving the audit trail while masking them from simulation). The review generalized it into a *testable* claim: purge materialized state, fold events over canon, and require an identical result.

### 3.4 Law versus state versus history, and the amendment path **[SOURCE]**

Runtime consequences do **not** rewrite canon immediately. The source's "two clocks":

```text
Simulation (Sn → Sn+1): a character reaches 0 HP / departs
        ↓
Locked episode output: conformed screenplay + atomic state_delta.json
        ↓
Archival gate: automated continuity check, then HUMAN gate (showrunner ratifies)
        ↓
Promotion into goblin-lore: frontmatter status: departed; dependent grievances
retired/mutated; compile.ts reseals bible-state.json (new version)
```

This is effectively **version-controlled constitutional amendment for a fictional universe.** *Runtime fact ≠ canonical fact* until it crosses the ratification boundary. The same path applies to permanent injuries, destroyed objects, revealed secrets, altered geography, and irreversible resource consumption.

### 3.5 Retired-entity semantics (resolving an apparent contradiction) **[REVIEW]**

The source says references to retired entities are invalid, yet preserves them in `_retired/` for auditability. Both are true once references are typed:

- **Active dependency → retired target: forbidden.**
- **Historical reference → retired target: permitted.**

A retired character receives tombstone metadata, including `banned_kinetic_verbs: ["*"]` so any generator attempting to animate a departed character trips Pass 1.

### 3.6 Unratified detail in projections **[REVIEW]**

Media projections inevitably invent detail (a wall's color, a gesture, a figure of speech). Unchecked, repetition turns invention into de facto canon. Three tiers:

1. **Discard**: ephemeral set dressing carries zero authority.
2. **Harvest queue**: recurrent details that anchor dramatic beats go to an `unratified_details` ledger for review.
3. **Formal ratification**: only showrunner approval promotes a detail into canon.

*Caveat:* detecting "recurrence" requires entity extraction over generated prose. Harvest will be partly manual unless that mechanism is specified.

## 4. The state vectors

### 4.1 The three vectors in the source **[SOURCE]**

| Vector | Question it answers | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Somatic** | What can the body physically do? | Per-limb condition, locomotion baseline, mobility rank, motor limitations, compiled `banned_kinetic_verbs`; acoustic/pulmonary constraints (e.g., pulmonary severity caps sentence length). |
| **Logistical** | What does the character hold, wear, carry? | Discrete equipment slots (`worn`, `held_primary`, `held_offhand`, `slung`, `cached`), mass accounting, conservation of physical matter. An item must have an owner or reside at an indexed location. |
| **Epistemic** | What does the character know, hide, refuse to say? | `known_secret_ids`, **Escalation Ceilings** (tripwires a character won't cross unprompted), `banned_attenuation_tokens` (words they cannot utter directly, forcing subtext through material proxies like tools or debts). |

The separation matters because generative systems collapse the three into one blurry sentence ("Bog is hurt and angry and has the spoon"). An injury changes *Somatic* capability; the spoon is *Logistical*; knowing who stole it is *Epistemic*.

### 4.2 A fourth vector **[REVIEW]**

**Relational/social state**: trust, debt, obligation, standing, leverage between *pairs*. A debt exists whether or not anyone remembers it. Separating relational ground truth from epistemic belief makes dramatic misunderstanding computable: *A believes B owes them; B believes the debt was settled.* The source already moves this way, requiring **directional** relationship vectors with `locked`/`shiftable` flags, a `repair_attempts` integer, and a `relationship_history` log.

### 4.3 Somatic mechanics in detail **[SOURCE]**

**Negative constraints beat prompts.** Instead of prompting "remember Bog can't use his right arm," an active `BRACHIAL_PLEXUS_RIGHT` condition compiles to a capability mask and a banned-verb set (`heave_two_handed`, `climb`, `vault`, `sprint`). The scene is *checked* against it, so correctness does not depend on the model recalling a sentence from thousands of tokens earlier.

**Hand-capacity invariant (as written):**

$$\sum_{s\in\text{slots}} s.\text{occupies\_hands} \le 2.0 - \text{hand\_impairment\_penalty}$$

Worked example: a right-arm plexus injury at severity 1.0 gives penalty 1.0, capacity drops from 2 to 1; a lantern (1 hand) plus a shotgun (1 hand) gives 2 ≰ 1 → `HAND_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, compile halts.

**Pre-flight feasibility.** The source also compiles capability into boolean clauses over a `SomaticCapabilityProfile` (per-limb enums like `PARALYZED | AMPUTATED | IMPAIRED | INTACT`, `mobility_rank` 1 to 4, held items), e.g. a two-handed valve requires `left_arm = INTACT ∧ right_arm = INTACT ∧ both hands empty`. Infeasible scenes are rejected at **T₀ for $0.00**, before any inference.

*Honest scoping:* at this size the "SAT" is trivially evaluated, not a general solver. Claims of "zero risk" should be scoped to the declared fields actually checked. If interacting constraints grow combinatorial, that changes.

**The source itself recommends per-limb tracking** over a single integer hand count, integrated with equip slots to model reach and handedness. This resolves the concern that `arms_available = 1.0` is too coarse.

### 4.4 The general principle behind the hand equation **[REVIEW]**

Narrative actions are constrained by **resource availability**. The same structure generalizes beyond hands to physical resources (legs, vision, breath), material resources (food, fuel, tools), cognitive resources (attention, knowledge, time), and social resources (trust, leverage, secrecy). A story compiler becomes a **resource-constrained state-transition system**, which is broader than a lore linter.

## 5. Epistemics: truth, belief, utterance

### 5.1 What the source contains **[SOURCE]**

Only the thin version: `known_secret_ids`, escalation ceilings, `banned_attenuation_tokens`, and the protected-unresolved-canon list.

### 5.2 What the review added **[REVIEW]** (not in the source)

**Truth ≠ belief ≠ utterance.** A character can say something true that they don't know is true; can know something and lie; can believe something false; can be deliberately ambiguous.

**Ignorance by omission.** A model cannot be told "pretend the character doesn't know X": mentioning X primes its use. Epistemic boundaries are enforced by **omitting the fact from that character's prompt context entirely.**

**A richer status vocabulary** than a boolean `known_secret`: `KNOWN_TRUE`, `KNOWN_FALSE`, `UNKNOWN`, `DISPUTED`, `FALSE_BELIEF`, `WITHHELD`, `UNRESOLVED_BY_CANON`.

**Unknown is indexed by observer.** "Unresolved by canon" (nobody, including the audience, gets the answer) is a *generation prohibition*. "Unknown to Bog but known to Spleen" is an ordinary information asymmetry. Conflating them makes the generator either leak answers it should hold or refuse to use information a character legitimately has. Even protected mysteries should have an explicit ratification path for resolution, so resolving one is deliberate rather than accidental.

**Belief records must be scoped to be checkable.** To compare belief with truth mechanically, restrict propositions to a **closed, enumerated set** of canonical fact IDs so "does Bog's belief match the world" is a lookup, not a semantic judgment. Two defects in the reviewed version to avoid:

- Truth is not boolean: a belief about an *unresolved-by-canon* fact can never be classed "correct" or "incorrect," and the system must not manufacture that classification.
- Thresholds (e.g., belief > 50%, assertion > 80%) are **policy choices**, must live in versioned configuration with a rationale, and must not be two unexplained numbers.

**Speech acts are assigned in the scene plan, before generation**, not self-labelled by the model. Better than an overlapping list (assert/bluff/deny/…) is an orthogonal pair: **sincerity** (does the utterance match the speaker's belief?) crossed with **content** (asserts, denies, questions, withholds, deflects). The linter then computes belief, computes utterance polarity, and compares mechanically.

**Hard versus soft.** Physical impossibility is hard. Epistemic violations are *nearly* hard, but must not forbid lucky guesses, bluffs, and false accusations; the correct predicate is that a character must not assert a hidden fact **with knowledge-level certainty or with details only knowledge could supply**. That predicate is hard to compute, which is exactly why omission (prevention) is primary and linting (detection) is a secondary net.

### 5.3 The audience layer **[REVIEW]**

Dramatic tension depends on the delta between what characters know and what the **audience** has been shown. Track audience knowledge separately (explicit on-screen, implied, withheld). It yields four pacing modes with distinct compile-time gates:

| Mode | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Dramatic irony** | Audience knows X; character does not. |
| **Suspense** | Audience knows a *time-bound threat* exists; character does not. |
| **Surprise** | X absent from both audience and character before the scene, present in both after. |
| **Mystery** | Character knows X; audience must not be told. |

*Note:* suspense and irony overlap; distinguish them by whether a time-bound threat exists. Any `Map`/`Set` used to store this must be serialized as sorted arrays to respect canonical serialization.

## 6. Causality

### 6.1 Grievances as state machines **[SOURCE]**

A grievance is not a plot point; it is an escalation ladder: surface conflict → escalation → higher tension → hidden cause exposed → new conflict unlocked. The silver-spoon dispute is an entry point into deeper structure (spoon → hoarding accusation → survival anxiety → famine history). The **"Therefore / But"** invariant (versus "and then") means each state creates the conditions for the next: plot as causal dependency, not chronological appointment.

### 6.2 The source declares the *whole* narrative graph acyclic **[SOURCE]**

Pass 2 asserts 𝒢 has no directed cycles, verified by three-color DFS (WHITE/GRAY/BLACK). Diamonds (multiple paths converging) are legal; cycles are fatal. The source's stated reasons are sound: simulation attractors and grudge loops, absorbing states with no exit, and infinite invalidation loops in dirty-propagation. It also names **epistemological calcification**: hastily authored facts accrue downstream dependents (in-degree 1 → 12 → 45) until a cycle would make them impossible to revise.

### 6.3 The correction **[REVIEW]**

Declaring the *entire* narrative graph acyclic would forbid legitimate recurrence: characters revisit locations, resentments, rituals, topics. The resolution:

- **Prerequisite/unlock graph** (structural unlocks, escalation ladders, world facts): **strictly acyclic.** Circular *prerequisites* deadlock.
- **Runtime interaction/relationship graph**: **may cycle.** Mutual influence is healthy.
- **Apparent causal loops unroll acyclically through time-indexing**: A at t₁ → B at t₂ → A at t₃ are three distinct nodes.

Two rules needed to make this sound: (a) apply acyclicity to *unlock dependencies*, not to *influence*; (b) **time-indexing fails for same-tick mutual dependency**, so an explicit intra-tick ordering rule is required (see §9.2).

## 7. Determinism and serialization

### 7.1 What the source establishes **[SOURCE]**

- **Byte-stable output.** Keys sorted recursively; **array order preserved** (arrays can encode causal sequence).
- **No volatile contamination.** `Date.now()`, `new Date().toISOString()`, `Math.random()`, network I/O, and `process.env` reads are forbidden in compilation; CI probes throw if they're touched. Temporal tracking uses narrative-logical clocks (integer cycles, episode boundaries, ordered events), never wall-clock time.
- **Atomic persistence** via POSIX rename (write temp, swap inode pointer) to prevent partial writes.
- **Hierarchical child seeding** so visual randomness never alters narrative outcomes.

### 7.2 Defects verified against the source **[VERIFIED]**

**The hash does not cover the whole artifact.** In `compileAndSealState`, `corpusHash` is computed over `sortedEntities` only; `schemaVersion` and `entityCount` are attached *outside* the hashed payload. If the goal is "this hash identifies the complete artifact," the envelope itself must be canonicalized and hashed. (Separately: a bare SHA-256 provides **integrity**, not **authenticity**; against deliberate replacement you need an external trust anchor or a signature.)

**The source is float-based.** Severities are `z.number().min(0.0).max(1.0)` and the hand equation uses `2.0 − penalty`. Floats create cross-platform serialization variance, in tension with the byte-stability the source demands elsewhere.

**Wall-clock leakage in the ledger.** The runtime table `grievance_runtime_state` contains `updated_at_ms INTEGER`. This does not violate the ban *for the compiled artifact*, but it does if the ledger is ever hashed or replayed. The ledger and the compiled artifact must be kept on opposite sides of the determinism boundary, or that column removed.

### 7.3 Proposed hardening **[REVIEW]**

| Concern | Rule |
|---|---|
| Floats | Store scalars as **fixed-point integers in basis points** (0 to 10,000). Reject `10000.0` at the parser level, since many parsers coerce silently. |
| Unicode | Normalize to NFC before key collation; sorting must not be locale-dependent. |
| Multiplication | Scale after **every** multiplication so intermediates never exceed 10⁸; do not carry growing products. |
| Rounding | Choose one policy and centralize it. Recommended: **round-half-even** in pure integer arithmetic; use floor-truncation only via a separately named function where conservative bias is intended (e.g., never overestimate capability). Truncation is not wrong, but it is a *policy*, not a default: it biases downward, so 1×1/10000 → 0 and repeated scaling collapses small values. |
| Signedness | State which quantities are signed/unsigned and which functions accept which. |

**A verified implementation hazard.** A round-half-even routine written with BigInt `/` and `%` is correct for **non-negative** inputs but silently mis-rounds **negatives** (division truncates toward zero and `%` takes the dividend's sign, so the "round up" branch never fires). Verified: `(−1) × 7500 / 10000` returns **0**; the correct result is **−1**. For non-negative inputs, the integer routine matched exact rational arithmetic on every tested pair (0 mismatches). Resolution: declare the function non-negative-only with a guard, and test it against an exact-rational reference. This bug exists because the signed/unsigned policy was unstated.

### 7.4 Randomness **[REVIEW]**

Randomness belongs in **simulation** (seeded), not in canon compilation (deterministic), and render randomness must be independent of both.

**Identity-keyed, not stream-position-keyed.** With one shared advancing RNG, inserting an unrelated conflict shifts every later draw. Instead derive each draw from stable *semantic* identity:

$$\text{Seed}_{\text{event}} = \text{KDF}(\text{MasterSeed},\ \text{domain},\ \text{identity})$$

- Identity must be a stable domain identifier (event, scene, resource, character ID), **never** an array index, loop counter, function name, or execution order, or a harmless refactor silently changes the narrative.
- **Encode components unambiguously.** Plain concatenation is ambiguous when identifiers can contain the separator (`"duel"‖"12"` collides with `"duel1"‖"2"`). Length-prefix each component or use a delimiter forbidden by the ID schema. (Contrast: `SHA256(H_{k−1} ‖ bytes)` is safe because `H_{k−1}` is fixed-width.)
- **Version the derivation scheme** (`seed_scheme_version`) and pin it with canon and compiler, because changing the KDF or label encoding changes every downstream draw.
- **Seed isolation is testable:** perturbing a render-layer seed must leave every simulation-layer output byte-identical.

### 8.2 The error in the source

`goblin-lore.md` line 5944 states the "corrected" margins as 11.3% at 0, 21.6% at ±1, 19.3% at ±2, with |margin| ≥ 6 at "roughly 15%." The first three are right. **The tail is wrong: P(|D| ≥ 6) = 10.80%, not ~15%.** (An early review reply endorsed the ~15% without checking; the corrected figure is the one that stands.)

### 8.3 Derived facts

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P(\|D\| ≤ 2) (near-parity contests) | 52.16% |
| P(D ≥ 1): strict win, ties count against the actor | 44.37% |
| P(D ≥ 0): win or tie | 55.63% |
| P(D ≥ 4) for **one named** actor (clean win) | 15.90% |
| P(\|D\| ≥ 4): **someone** wins cleanly | 31.79% |

**More than half of all unmodified contests are decided within two points of parity.** Mutual wounds and costly compromises are the statistical baseline, which is why small modifiers are not decorative.

**Precision rule.** *A correct number attached to an imprecise proposition is still a defective specification.* "Clean win" for a named actor (15.90%) and for either side (31.79%) are different events. Likewise the baseline win rate is **not 50%**: with ties possible it is 44.37% under a strict-win predicate. Always define the resolution predicate first (`Win(M) ⇔ D + M ≥ 1`) and derive every probability from it.

Observations:

- **The marginal gain of each modifier point equals the probability mass of exactly one margin value.** Gains peak at ≈11.3 points at the center (M = 0 → +1) and shrink to ≈4.3 at M = +6.
- The table is **not symmetric** around 50%, because ties go against the actor.

- With a total clamp of [−2, +2], strict-win probability ranges from 23.92% to 66.44%, a swing of **42.52 points** (an earlier draft's 44.14 was wrong).
- Each of these is a **chosen policy number**, not a derivable one: the clamp, the 40% closure threshold, 24 fps, retry counts.

### 8.5 Fixed-target versus opposed sensitivity

| Target (2d6 ≥ t) | Ways of 36 | P(success) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 33 | 91.67% |
| 5 | 30 | 83.33% |
| 6 | 26 | 72.22% |
| 7 | 21 | 58.33% |
| 8 | 15 | 41.67% |
| 9 | 10 | 27.78% |
| 10 | 6 | 16.67% |
| 11 | 3 | 8.33% |
| 12 | 1 | 2.78% |

A one-step change in a fixed 2d6 target moves probability by the mass on the crossed total: 16.67 points at 7↔8, but only 2.78 at 11↔12. So the accurate rule is **sensitivity is a function of the operating point.** At comparable baselines (~42 to 44% success), a fixed-target +1 (7→8: 16.67 pts) is roughly **1.5×** an opposed +1 (11.27 pts), but that ratio holds only near the median of 2d6 and inverts in the tails. Compute leverage at every operating point you actually ship.

### 8.6 An earlier arithmetic error, for the record

A draft claimed a +3 modifier gives 81.64%. The correct value is **76.08%** (986/1296). And "a +1 shift moves probability 12 to 17 points" is wrong for opposed contests (10.80 to 11.27 points; the 12 to 17 range applies only to fixed targets near the median).

## 9. Execution, time, and concurrency

### 9.1 The spatiotemporal hierarchy **[SOURCE]**

Narrative units and presentation units stay separate:

```text
L1  Simulation Cycle   world clock tick; moves agents; advances cooldowns
L2  Episode            packaged dramatic broadcast unit
L3  Scene              bounded spatial encounter (one location, fixed cast)
L4  Beat (semantic)    narrative turn: accusation, revelation, compromise
L5  Shot (visual)      one camera angle, framing, or reaction insert
L6  Frame (temporal)   audio duration (ms) quantized to integer frames
```

One beat can yield several shots (Accusation → speaker, reaction cut, insert of a clenched fist); camera cuts must not fragment story units.

### 9.2 Two-phase tick commit **[REVIEW]**

To eliminate order-of-execution bias when multiple agents act in one cycle:

1. **Read & propose**: all agents read the *frozen* tick-start state and submit intended actions.
2. **Arbitrate & commit**: the engine resolves contested resources via opposed mechanics and applies all deltas atomically; the clock advances for everyone simultaneously.

Two details must be specified for this to be actually deterministic:

- **Arbitration order must be deterministic even when outcomes are random.** Resolve conflicts in a fixed order (e.g., sorted by contested-resource ID) and key each conflict's draws to `(tick, resource_id)`, so adding an unrelated agent changes no other roll.
- **Chained intra-tick dependencies** (A hands B a wrench while C disarms B) cannot be expressed against frozen state. Either forbid such proposals or define a second sub-phase. Leaving it implicit is hidden state, and hidden state destroys reproducibility.

**Void is an outcome.** A contest whose precondition vanishes before arbitration (the wrench is destroyed) yields `CONTEST_VOID`, not a win for either side. The event algebra should be **total** over all legal transitions, including non-resolution, and totality is property-testable (`apply` never throws on valid inputs).

### 9.3 Frame budgeting from audio **[REVIEW]**

Audio duration is the master clock; video conforms to it. Converting each line with `⌈D_ms/1000 × 24⌉` accumulates rounding drift. Compute from **cumulative milliseconds**:

$$\text{Start}_k = \left\lceil \tfrac{\sum_{i<k} D_i}{1000}\cdot \text{fps}\right\rceil,\qquad \text{End}_k = \left\lceil \tfrac{\sum_{i\le k} D_i}{1000}\cdot \text{fps}\right\rceil - 1$$

Then `End_k + 1 = Start_{k+1}` by construction: lines tile the timeline with **no gaps and no overlaps**, and total error is bounded below one frame regardless of line count. Measured by Monte-Carlo simulation (20,000 trials per row, line durations uniform over 300 to 4,000 ms):

| Lines in scene | Per-line ceiling: mean drift / max (frames) | Cumulative: mean drift / max (frames) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.50 / 1.00 | 0.50 / 1.00 |
| 6 | 3.00 / 5.38 | 0.50 / 1.00 |
| 12 | 6.01 / 10.09 | 0.50 / 1.00 |
| 24 | 11.97 / 17.06 | 0.50 / 1.00 |

Per-line ceilings drift ≈0.5 frame per line (a 12-line scene: about 6 frames on average, up to about 10 in the worst sampled case, so an earlier "6 to 12 frames" is a fair average-to-worst range, not a typical value).

**Loose ends.**
- **Sub-frame lines.** At 24 fps a frame is ≈41.67 ms. If a line is shorter than a frame, `End_k = Start_k − 1`, an empty interval. Policy: pad to a one-frame minimum, or merge into the neighbor. **The padded duration, not the measured one, must be the recorded value** (keeping the original measurement alongside), or audio and frame budget disagree.
- **Frame rate as data.** Store it as a rational `p/q` and compute in integers (23.976 = 24000/1001), so a rate change is configuration, and the minimum span is `⌈1000·q/p⌉` ms, not a hard-coded 42.

## 10. Verification: prevention versus detection

### 10.1 The dual-field gate **[SOURCE]**

A model can emit `extracted_motor_actions: ["LIMP"]` while its prose says *"he sprinted across the room."* Self-reported structure is therefore treated as an **untrusted claim**. The source's gate checks the structured action field and, independently, the prose.

### 10.2 The tiered stack **[SOURCE + REVIEW]**

1. **Grammar-constrained decoding** (GBNF) restricts the action field to valid capability enums.
2. **Negation-aware lemma scan** over prose using dependency parsing (*"he couldn't run"* passes; *"he ran"* halts).
3. **Independent extraction pass** by a second model at temperature 0.
4. **Capability-bounded discrepancy gate**: reject when *either* signal violates a constrained capability:

$$(\text{Actions}_{\text{self}} \cup \text{Actions}_{\text{extracted}}) \cap \text{BannedVerbs} \neq \emptyset \Rightarrow \text{REJECT}$$

Requiring the two sets to be **equal** is the wrong design: it halts on harmless disagreements ("glance" vs "look"). Gate on **mask violations in either set**, and ignore unconstrained noise. The source itself reaches this conclusion (line 5947).

### 10.3 Limits that must be designed around **[REVIEW]**

- **Prose checking is the weak link.** Keywords miss paraphrase ("bolted," "dashed"); a second model reintroduces model unreliability. Mitigate upstream: have the model *select from a legal action menu* rather than freely author actions.
- **Temperature 0 is not determinism.** Batching and GPU floating-point can still vary output. The extraction pass is a probabilistic check and must sit **outside** the deterministic boundary, or its output must be recorded as an event.
- **The action vocabulary must be a governed, versioned ontology.** Both generator and verifier must map into one closed vocabulary with synonym sets (`SPRINT`: bolt, dash, race…), each action carrying semantic requirements (limbs, mobility rank). Ship a **golden corpus** of prose sentences with labeled canonical actions and track **precision and recall**, since the linter is a classifier and should be measured like one.
- **Omission tests prevent direct injection only.** Absence of a string does not prevent *inference* from remaining context. Treat a string test as necessary but not sufficient; add a periodic, non-blocking evaluation that probes whether a model can reconstruct omitted facts. Test the *specific omitted proposition's* tokens, not a common word (`/famine/i` would wrongly fail a character who legitimately knows a famine occurred but not its origin).

### 10.4 The sorting rule

For every guarantee, ask: **is it enforced by construction, or by a fallible detector?** Use it as a table.

| Guarantee | Mechanism | Class |
|---|---|---|
| No banned action in the action field | Grammar-constrained decoding | **Prevention** |
| No banned action in prose | Lemma scan + extraction | **Detection** (nonzero miss rate) |
| Fact absent from prompt | Context assembly | **Prevention** (direct injection only) |
| Fact not inferred from context | Leak evaluation | **Detection** (statistical) |
| Same state from same events | Pure, total fold | **Prevention** (by construction) |
| Physical capability limits | Pre-flight arithmetic | **Prevention** (declared fields only) |
| Voice consistency | Stylometric scoring | **Detection** (advisory) |

Whenever prose says "this guarantees X," ask whether X is *mathematically enforced* or *tested by a fallible detector*. That single question exposes most remaining overstatements. Replace "zero risk" with "guaranteed by construction for property X" and name X.

## 11. Governance, provenance, and lifecycle

### 11.1 Human gates are events **[REVIEW]**

Record `CANON_RATIFICATION`, `PARDON_GRANTED`, `HARVEST_ACCEPTED`, `RENDER_APPROVED`, `SCENE_REJECTED` as immutable events, not ephemeral UI actions. The machine can prove an approval was *recorded*; it cannot prove the approval was *wise*. For each gate specify: what the reviewer sees, what they may decide, what is recorded, what happens while pending (halt or speculate), what happens on timeout, and how rejection rolls back. **Throughput matters:** an auditable gate that receives more decisions than a human can process becomes a rubber stamp, so specify batching and priority.

### 11.2 Two boundaries for state change **[REVIEW]**

Runtime state changes pass through the **event boundary**. Canon and schema changes pass through the **versioned migration boundary**. These are the only two. No privileged side channel: a waiver that injects a state change *is* an event and must obey append-time validation, sequencing, replay, and audit.

### 11.3 The tiered constraint model and scoped pardons **[REVIEW]**

Not every invariant deserves the same enforcement.

| Tier | Contents | Policy |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard** | Physical anatomy, limb counts, inventory conservation, death, spatial occupancy; and (nearly) epistemic access | Violation fails the build; **unpardonable**. A waiver cannot let a paralyzed arm lift a heavy tool. |
| **Soft** | Voice cadence, refusal vectors, sentence length, behavioral tics, relational tendencies | Scored; pardonable. |

A pardon must be **scoped** to an exact `(episode, scene, character)` tuple, carry a justification, be content-addressed (no calendar dates embedded in IDs, which would feed non-determinism), and reject any standing or perpetual form. A scene-scoped pardon does not also need a cycle-based expiry; pick one lifetime model. A pardon that injects a consequence is a state-mutating event.

*Why pardons exist at all:* a system with no exception mechanism can only produce what its rules foresee. Consistency must not smother surprise.

### 11.4 Waivers and explicit absence **[SOURCE]**

Asset statuses `INTENTIONALLY_ABSENT`, `WAIVED`, `VALID` in a central waiver ledger distinguish **deliberate null variants from broken paths**. Generalize the distinction: `UNKNOWN` (deliberately not known) ≠ `BROKEN` (the system failed to determine) ≠ `ABSENT` ≠ `INTENTIONALLY_ABSENT` ≠ `WAIVED`.

### 11.5 Provenance **[REVIEW]**

Every permanent fact should answer *where did this come from?* (origin event, episode, rule version, approving gate, canon revision). Then `render → scene → dialogue → event → canonical fact → ratification` is traceable. For every nondeterministic external operation, record the operation ID, input hash, model/voice identity and version, prompt/settings hash, output, and output hash.

### 11.6 Versioning several things, not one **[REVIEW]**

`world_version`, `schema_version`, `rule_version`, `action_vocabulary_version`, `renderer_version`, `seed_scheme_version`, `compiler_build_hash`. A 2028 compiler must not interpret a 2026 event under 2028 semantics. **Pin each episode to both canon revision and compiler build**, and run *historical mode* against the pinned compiler (preserve or containerize old builds).

**Canon migration.** Migration functions μ₁→₂ must be **total** (every valid v1 state maps to a valid v2 state), verified by replaying historical event streams through the adapter. Test separately that (a) every historical episode replays under its pinned revision and (b) μ yields valid v2 states. Remastered episodes render to explicitly tagged variants; the original master is preserved.

**Ledger schema evolution.** Wrap events in a versioned envelope with pure **upcaster** functions so old events remain readable as event definitions change (distinct from canon migration).

### 11.7 Health versus validity **[SOURCE]**

*The compiler asks "is this legal?" Telemetry asks "is this alive?"* A corpus can be valid but boring, unbalanced, overcoupled, or full of dead lore. Source metrics:

- **Dyadic coverage:** N characters give C(N,2) channels (N = 4 → 6). Active grievances per dyad; warn below a minimum (G_min < 3). A zero-degree pair means the lottery cannot generate that two-character scene without fallbacks.
- **Leverage asymmetry:** flag directed string ratios above 4:1; the weaker side has no counter-play currency in duels.
- **Escalation pacing audit:** if a macro-arc reveal is due at cycle 32 and the seed grievance starts around cycle 4, a 4-stage ladder needs a stage roughly every 7 cycles; cooldown and decay parameters can make the target *provably unreachable*, and the audit flags that.
- **Graph centrality:** dead lore (zero in-degree tail) versus monolithic spines (hyper-concentration).
- **Two-number honesty:** record both automated detections and human-audited misses to expose tooling blind spots. The **"Still Good" protocol** (`last_reconsidered_at` prioritized by downstream in-degree, not calendar dates) fights calcification.

**Guardrails.** Health metrics are **diagnostics for a human, not optimization targets**; optimizing them (Goodhart's law) invites generators to fabricate conflicts to lift a score. Also note the source's dyad table (18/8/5/3/1/0) is an *illustrative* example that sums to 35, not derived data. And an "override rate" is the honest name for the review's proposed metric: **overrides ÷ rejections**, not ÷ all invocations (a rule that rejects 10% of scenes and is wrong every time would otherwise read as 10%), and it is biased low when reviewers are fatigued, so pair it with labeled evaluation data.

## 12. Continuity as cache invalidation **[SOURCE]**

Narrative continuity behaves like a build system. Each scene carries a dependency fingerprint:

$$\text{SceneHash} = \mathcal{H}\Big(\text{AST} + \sum_i \mathcal{H}(\text{Dependency}_i) + \text{Assets} + \text{CompilerVersion}\Big)$$

When an upstream fact changes, a forward sweep along the DAG (a recursive CTE in the D1/SQLite ledger) marks downstream scenes dirty (`sower why-dirty` reports the blast radius and rebuild cost). Because the prerequisite graph is proven acyclic, the cascade is **finite and unidirectional**, protecting against infinite invalidation and regeneration storms.

**Asymmetric minimal-enclosing-unit repair.** *Backward sweep* collects active constraints (e.g., who holds a key) without widening the rewrite; *forward sweep* expands only if an entity introduced by the patch is referenced later; a **seam gate** re-audits the joined text for coreference, pronoun clarity, and tense. Closures exceeding **40% of a scene** escalate to a human rewrite (a policy number, so it belongs in versioned config).

## 13. Replay, simulation, and rendering are different operations **[REVIEW]**

The most valuable implementation-level distinction to emerge:

| Operation | Meaning | External systems | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Replay** | Reconstruct what actually happened | **None**; consumes recorded results | Replay(H) → S |
| **Re-simulate** | Run the rules again under new seed/config | Legitimately produces something new | Simulate(S₀, σ, θ) → H′ |
| **Re-render** | Project established history into a new medium | Consumes recorded intermediates | Render(H, ρ) → A |

Without this, "replay" degrades into "run the same algorithms again and hope."

**Recorded side effects are authoritative.** Model outputs, TTS-measured durations, and human approvals must be recorded as events. Re-querying a model at temperature 0 is *re-execution*, not replay. If TTS returns 1837 ms today and 1829 ms after an upgrade, an unrecorded duration silently rewrites the frame layout of an otherwise identical episode.

**Crash windows and idempotency.** If a worker crashes after a model returns but before the result is recorded, a retry yields a *different* result. Rule: **a result is authoritative only once durably recorded; an unrecorded attempt is discarded.** Give each operation an idempotency key so a result recorded twice is deduplicated, not double-applied. Retry attempts, their failure reasons, and the *inputs* used (a negative-constraint feedback changes the prompt) must also be recorded so replay can explain why the accepted result was accepted.

**A re-render must be able to consume recorded intermediates** (approved dialogue, recorded audio, measured duration). If it re-invokes TTS it has silently become a partial re-simulation.

**Re-render invariance contract.**

| Preserved (must match exactly) | Allowed to vary |
|---|---|
| Frame budget; shot cut boundaries; audio stem hashes; character presence and screen side; dialogue AST snapshot hash | Pixel-level diffusion noise; lighting bloom and grain; minor cloth/hair physics; background dust |

**Do not require the render hash to differ.** A renderer that happens to be bit-identical on a given machine is still correct. Assert that preserved invariants match; treat hash change as informative. Likewise a test that noise changes the output verifies the noise input *reaches the renderer*, not that outputs must differ.

**Determinism, replayability, reproducibility, integrity, provenance are five different properties.** A system can have logical state deterministic ✓, historical replay reproducible ✓, GPU render bit-identical ✗, approved video archived ✓, provenance recorded ✓. That is not a failure; it is a correctly characterized system.

## 14. Storage and durability **[REVIEW]**

Media is **non-reproducible** across GPUs, driver patches, and container revisions. The earlier claim "every projection can be regenerated" is too strong: regenerable in principle from state, not to the same pixels. Reproducibility protects *derivations*; archival protects *history*.

| Artifact class | Source of truth? | Rebuildable from | Policy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canon files | Yes | nothing | Version control, signed tags, offsite mirrors |
| Event log | Yes | nothing | Append-only, hash-chained, replicated |
| **Recorded external results** (model/TTS outputs, ASTs) | Yes | nothing | Content-addressed, write-once; **the easily-forgotten class** |
| Approved masters | Yes (evidence) | nothing | Content-addressed cold storage, periodic re-hash |
| Materialized state | No | canon + events | No backup; rebuild |
| Dependency graph, indexes | No | canon + events | In-memory; regenerated |
| Unapproved drafts, infill caches | No | re-run | TTL purge |

**Retention conflict.** A time-based purge must never expire a blob **referenced by any event**; add a reference check to the purge job.

**Log structure.** The authoritative log is **strictly linear**: `H_k = SHA256(H_{k−1} ‖ canonical(E_k))`, with the hash covering the *canonical* serialization. Speculative branches (what-ifs, alternate takes) live in **separate non-authoritative namespaces** carrying `forked_from_event_hash`. **Promotion requires a fast-forward rule** (the branch forked from the *current* head); otherwise re-simulate or revalidate, because the branch's events were generated against a state that may no longer exist. A general hash-DAG for the main log is unnecessary complexity.

**Sequence numbers.** Require **monotonic and unique**, not "gapless." Gaplessness under concurrent writers and rollbacks is hard, and replay doesn't need it. A hash chain detects loss, reorder, and tamper better than a gapless integer, and composes with content-addressed storage.

## 15. Test strategy

### 15.1 The tracer bullet **[SOURCE recommendation, REVIEW design]**

The source's own conclusion is to *stop expanding architecture and prove one artifact end to end*: one character, one grievance, one line, one shot, one audio stem. Stage it so nondeterminism is isolated:

- **Stage A, pure logic:** stub model and stub renderer returning fixed recorded values. Tests every deterministic claim with **zero flakiness** (canon → event → fold → capability check → seeded randomness → frame arithmetic).
- **Stage B, real side effects:** real model and TTS, **outputs recorded**; verify replay makes no external calls.
- **Stage C, rendering:** real render, archived by content hash; a second render may differ, the ledger may not.

### 15.2 The five assertions **[REVIEW]**

1. **Deterministic replay:** same seed and canon yield byte-identical event-log and dialogue-AST hashes.
2. **Pre-flight rejection at T₀:** an infeasible action fails with **zero model calls**. Assert the *mechanism*, not a symptom: a mock with call count 0 **and** a sandbox with outbound network unbound and credentials scrubbed (a cost field can read zero by accident; a counter misses a second client).
3. **Seed isolation:** perturbing render noise leaves the narrative AST and frame budget unchanged.
4. **Deletion principle:** purge materialized state, fold events over canon, require exact equality. **Corollary:** delete recorded external results and require replay to **fail loudly, naming the missing operation IDs**, rather than silently re-invoking a model.
5. **Ignorance by omission:** the assembled prompt for a character who doesn't know fact X contains neither X's identifier nor its canonical paraphrases.

### 15.3 Beyond fixtures **[REVIEW]**

- **Property-based tests** over generated event histories: determinism; **prefix equivalence** `Fold(Fold(S,A),B) = Fold(S, A‖B)`; totality (no unhandled exceptions); rejected events never enter authoritative history; projections cannot mutate the log.
- **Test the canonicalizer first,** adversarially (key order, equivalent Unicode forms, `10000` vs `10000.0`), before anything hashed depends on it.
- **A generated document.** Regenerate quantitative sections from code and **fail the build if committed markdown differs.** *Derivable numbers are generated; chosen numbers live in versioned config with a stated reason.* Three categories: mathematical invariants (provable), software invariants (testable), policy choices (not provable; a test proves the implementation obeys ±2, not that ±2 is narratively right).
- **Contract tests at each seam.** Remaining defects live at interfaces, not in algorithms: modifier system ↔ probability model; waiver ↔ time; waiver ↔ state mutation; canon ↔ runtime; event log ↔ replay; model call ↔ determinism; TTS ↔ frame arithmetic; integer policy ↔ actual examples; human approval ↔ machine state.
- **Pipeline health.** A pipeline that halts on every ambiguity starves. Bounded retries (e.g., two, with the violation fed back as a negative constraint), a circuit breaker after consecutive failures with an escalation ticket, and per-rule override rates.

## 18. The deepest observation

The project is trying to make a narrative universe behave like a physical one. In a physical universe, gravity does not politely remind an astronaut *"please remember you cannot fly."* It simply makes certain trajectories unavailable. A character with a destroyed arm should not receive a reminder in a prompt; the injury should change the set of states the character can occupy. A secret is not "something the writer knows"; it is a fact in an epistemic graph. A possession is not mentioned in prose; it occupies a location and belongs to someone. A grievance is not a paragraph; it is a causal state machine. A death is not dialogue; it is an event that must eventually alter canonical state. A screenplay is not the world; it is a projection of it.

The review's corollary for the specification itself: **every place where language claims more than a mechanism delivers is a bug waiting to happen.** The fix has the same shape everywhere: *narrow the domain until the mechanism is genuinely sound* (closed proposition sets instead of free semantics; plan-assigned labels instead of model-assigned ones; a linear chain instead of a general graph), *and generate or test whatever can be generated or tested.*

The architecture is coherent. The remaining risk is operational, not conceptual: whether the pipeline can run, reject, retry, and recover often enough to finish an episode. That cannot be answered on paper. **Run the staged tracer bullet next, and let the machine find the bugs faster than another round of reading.**
