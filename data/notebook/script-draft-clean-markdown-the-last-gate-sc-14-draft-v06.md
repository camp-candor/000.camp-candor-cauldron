# THE LAST GATE
*Scene SC-14 — Draft v06*

---

### Logline
An aging traveler, once known by a grand name now worn thin by years on the road, arrives at the last checkpoint before the plaza's edge — and, through his own insistence on being recognized, talks himself directly into the one admission he came here to avoid.

### Theme
Being technically who you once were is not the same as being someone now.

## Setting

*A checkpoint of indeterminate origin — part customs booth, part confessional. Weathered stone, peeling gilt paint suggesting former importance. A gatekeeper sits behind a low counter, unbothered by time. Dust and heat. The traveler arrives carrying almost nothing.*

---

## Beat One — Arrival

*The Traveler, once impeccably dressed, now in clothes that were fine a decade and several roads ago, approaches with the practiced posture of someone who has arrived at many counters before.*

**TRAVELER**  
Good evening. I believe I'm expected.

*The Gatekeeper, without looking up, turns a page in a ledger that seems to have no end.*

**GATEKEEPER**  
Everyone is expected. That's not the same as being permitted.

---

## Beat Two — The Empty Folio

**TRAVELER** *(a small, practiced laugh)*  
Naturally. I have my papers.

*He produces an ornate, sun-bleached folio. The Gatekeeper accepts it without interest, opens it, finds it empty, sets it down as one might set down something already known to be broken.*

**GATEKEEPER**  
These are very handsome papers.

**TRAVELER**  
They were drawn up by the finest office in the capital.

**GATEKEEPER**  
I don't doubt it. They're also blank.

*(a beat)*

What else have you brought?

---

## Beat Three — The Trap He Sets Himself

*The Traveler hesitates for the first time — searches his coat, his memory, the horizon behind him, as though the answer might be found in any of those places.*

**TRAVELER**  
I brought... my name. Surely that still carries weight somewhere on this road.

**GATEKEEPER**  
It carries exactly as much weight as anyone in the plaza still chooses to lend it. Ask them yourself, if you like. I only stamp what's real.

---

## Beat Four — The Confession

**TRAVELER** *(quieter now, the performance slipping)*  
I was received once. In rooms far grander than this one. I was toasted. I was — *(he stops himself)* — I was somebody people wrote about.

**GATEKEEPER** *(not unkindly)*  
I believe you. I'm not asking who you were. I'm asking what you're carrying now, standing at my counter, tonight.

*A long silence. The Traveler sets down his folio, empty as it is, as though laying down something heavier.*

---

## Beat Five — The Name, Returned

**TRAVELER** *(barely audible)*  
Nothing. I'm carrying nothing.

**GATEKEEPER** *(gently, closing the ledger)*  
Good. Now we can begin honestly.

*(a beat, almost kind)*

You said your name still carries weight. I'll tell you plainly what it's worth here: exactly as much as this — *(taps the empty folio)* — until you decide to be someone standing in front of me instead of someone written about somewhere else.

*The Gatekeeper reaches beneath the counter and produces a single plain token, unremarkable, unadorned, and sets it in front of the Traveler.*

**GATEKEEPER**  
This won't get you through the gate. But it'll get you a seat, a drink, and an evening where no one asks you to be anyone at all.

*The Traveler looks at it a long moment. Then picks it up — not gratefully, not bitterly, simply because there is nothing else left to do with his hands.*

**TRAVELER** *(a faint, real smile)*  
I suppose that will have to be enough.

**GATEKEEPER**  
It usually is, in the end.

*The Traveler turns and walks toward the plaza's edge, no longer carrying the folio at all. He leaves it on the counter. The Gatekeeper does not call after him.*

*Lights fade.*

---

## Production Notes

* **Staging:** Simultaneously bureaucratic and dreamlike; no clear indication of nation, era, or authority.
* **Costume:** Once-fine, meticulously maintained despite wear — dignity as an act of will.
* **Sound:** Minimal underscore; let silence between lines carry weight.
* **Lighting:** Warm but failing light, dimming slightly across the scene so the fade feels earned.
* **Callback Discipline:** Only the "my name" line is paid off. Resist adding a second thread — the scene's intimacy depends on smallness.
