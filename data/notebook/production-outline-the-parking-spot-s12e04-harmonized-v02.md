### 🎭 KEY THEME
> *Being technically correct is not the same as being right, and Larry will never understand the difference.*

### 📝 LOGLINE
> Larry takes a parking spot he insists he "called" with his blinker — a decision that spirals into a neighborhood dispute, drags in a favor he owes Jeff, and ends with Larry losing the moral high ground he never actually had.

# ACT I — The Spot

### 🎨 Tone & Register
* **Visuals:** Flat, sitcom-naturalistic — no stylization, the show's whole aesthetic is "no aesthetic."
* **Audio:** The bass-and-pizzicato theme sting on cuts; otherwise entirely diegetic — no score under scenes.

---

## `BEAT 1` — The Blinker Defense  
**Pacing:** `SETUP` · **Discomfort:** `2/10`

- **What happens:**  
  Larry has his blinker on, waiting for a parking spot. Another driver swoops in first. Larry gets out to confront him on principle, not because he needed the spot urgently.
- **Audience vs. Larry knowledge:**  
  - Audience: 😬 *already knows this is a bad hill to die on*  
  - Larry: 😤 *fully convinced he's right*

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`**  
> The scene must establish "the blinker rule" as Larry's genuinely-held, arguable-but-absurd principle — the actual dialogue is unscripted, but the *premise stated aloud* by Larry is load-bearing for every later callback.

> [!TIP]
> **IMPROV ANCHOR `[SUGGESTED]`**  
> Let the other driver be reasonable, not a cartoon villain — funnier if Larry is arguing against someone with a fair point.

### If this act isn't landing:
> If the confrontation feels too aggressive too fast, slow it down — the comedy lives in Larry escalating a *civil* disagreement past the point anyone else wants to keep having it, not in shouting matches.

# ACT II — Two Threads

### 🎨 Tone & Register
> Cutting between two ostensibly unrelated tracks — the parking beef, and a small favor Larry owes Jeff.

---

## `BEAT 2` — Public Litigation  
**Pacing:** `ESCALATION` · **Discomfort:** `4/10`

- **What happens:**  
  Larry brings the parking dispute to the neighborhood, trying to recruit witnesses to validate the blinker rule. Reactions are mixed, mostly unsympathetic.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`**  
> At least one neighbor must express visible embarrassment *for* Larry rather than *at* him — the show's specific flavor of cringe requires secondhand discomfort, not just direct conflict.

---

## `BEAT 3` — The B-Story Seed  
**Pacing:** `PARALLEL SETUP` · **Discomfort:** `5/10`

- **What happens:**  
  Jeff asks Larry for a small, awkward favor — cover for him at a family event. Larry agrees, distracted, without really listening to the terms.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`**  
> Larry's agreement must be genuinely careless — this is the setup for `Beat 4`'s callback, so the audience needs to register he wasn't paying attention, without Larry registering it himself.

### If this act isn't landing:
> If the two threads feel disconnected rather than parallel, don't force an explicit connection yet — trust the format; the collision belongs in Act III, not here.

# ACT III — Collision

### 🎨 Tone & Register
> Everything tightens — physical spaces get smaller, scenes get more crowded with people who all have opinions.

---

## `BEAT 4` — The Blinker Rule, Weaponized  
**Pacing:** `SHARP TURN` · **Discomfort:** `7/10` · 🔁 **CALLBACK**

- **What happens:**  
  Someone uses Larry's own "blinker rule" against him in an unrelated context — he objects, only to be reminded, publicly, that he invented the standard himself.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`**  
> This must land as Larry being hoisted by his own petard specifically — the callback only works if the audience clearly remembers `Beat 1`'s stated rule.

---

## `BEAT 5` — The Favor Backfires  
**Pacing:** `ATTEMPTED FIX` · **Discomfort:** `6/10`

- **What happens:**  
  Larry tries to make good on Jeff's favor, but because he wasn't listening in `Beat 3`, he gets the terms wrong in a way that makes things worse, not better.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`**  
> Larry's attempted fix must be sincere, not malicious — the comedy is incompetence and inattention, not bad faith.

### If this act isn't landing:
> If the two threads still feel like separate episodes rather than one, pull the collision earlier — a fully braided episode usually has the threads touching by the midpoint, not just at the climax.

# ACT IV — The Collision Point

### 🎨 Tone & Register
> Maximum density — as many aggrieved parties in one space as the scene can hold.

---

## `BEAT 6` — Everyone, At Once  
**Pacing:** `CLIMAX` · **Discomfort:** `9/10` — **PEAK** · 🔁 **CALLBACK**

- **What happens:**  
  The parking neighbor, Jeff, and the favor's other party all end up in the same room. Larry's blinker rule and his careless favor collide, and he's forced to defend both simultaneously, with no good answer for either.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`**  
> Larry must be given no clean exit line — the peak works specifically because he doesn't get the last word.

# ACT V — Anti-Resolution

### 🎨 Tone & Register
> Deflated, quiet by the show's standards — no music swell, no lesson, credits roll on something faintly humiliating.

---

## `BEAT 7` — Nothing Learned  
**Discomfort:** `3/10` · 🔁 **CALLBACK**

- **What happens:**  
  Larry, alone or with Jeff, briefly seems to concede a point — then immediately reasserts the blinker rule in some unrelated new context, signaling he's learned genuinely nothing.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`**  
> The final beat must NOT resolve the theme — the ending confirms the premise (Larry doesn't change) rather than completing an arc.
