### KEY THEME
> *Debts don't erase danger — they just mean you find out together.*

### LOGLINE
> Jack returns to the swamp for a third crossing and finds the creature waiting for him **deliberately** — not as a threat, but as a warning: something upstream has been poisoning the water, and it's dying along with everything else that drinks from it.

# ACT I — The Third Crossing

### Tone & Palette
* **Visuals:** The familiar swamp palette, but subtly *wrong* — a faint sickly sheen on the water's surface, dulled greens starting to yellow at the edges of the frame.
* **Audio:** Ambient sound present but thinner than either previous episode — fewer frogs, fewer insects, without yet calling attention to why.

---

## Beat 1 — Return
**Pacing:** `SLOW / HOLD`

* **What happens:** Jack re-enters the swamp along a different route than before, clearly intending simple passage, nothing more. He notices the diminished wildlife sound but doesn't yet connect it to anything.
* **Audience vs. Jack knowledge:**
  * Audience: Unknown
  * Jack: Unknown
  * *(Plants unease without explaining it)*
* **BOARD NOTE `[REQUIRED]`:** The sickly water sheen must be present but subtle enough to be missed on a first viewing — this is a detail that should **reward attention**, not announce itself.
* **BOARD NOTE `[SUGGESTED]`:** Echo the low-horizon, water-dominant framing from `501` and `502` to keep visual continuity across the trilogy.

---

## Beat 2 — Waiting
**Pacing:** `BUILD`

* **What happens:** The creature is already there when Jack arrives — not stalking, not hidden, simply waiting in open water at the crossing point, watching him approach. **This is new behavior** — it has never let itself be seen this plainly before.
* **Audience vs. Jack knowledge:**
  * Audience: Recognizes the shift
  * Jack: Registers it too, and stops
* **BOARD NOTE `[REQUIRED]`:** No aggression in the creature's posture or framing here — the visual grammar must clearly read as *"waiting for,"* not *"hunting."*
* **Dialogue:** `NONE`

### If this act isn't landing:
> If the shift in the creature's behavior isn't reading as significant, **do not** add dialogue to explain it — instead cut back briefly to a visual callback of its posture from `501`'s ambush, so the contrast does the explaining.

---

# ACT II — What It's Showing Him

### Tone & Palette
* **Visuals:** The sickly yellow tint from Act I intensifies as Jack moves upstream — clearer, more disturbing signs of contamination: dead fish, discolored roots, a filmy residue on standing water.
* **Audio:** Near-total silence returns, but this time it reads as *absence of life* rather than tension — a colder, sadder quiet than either previous episode's silence.

---

## Beat 3 — The Lead
**Pacing:** `MICRO-TENSION`

* **What happens:** The creature moves a short distance upstream and stops, looking back at Jack — an unmistakable invitation to follow. After a pause, he does.
* **Audience vs. Jack knowledge:**
  * Both: Trust now flowing in the opposite direction
* **BOARD NOTE `[REQUIRED]`:** No dialogue or internal monologue — the "invitation" must be readable purely through the creature's repeated look-back, a specific, simple animation beat that should recur **exactly this way at least twice**.

---

## Beat 4 — The Source
**Pacing:** `THE HOLD`

* **What happens:** They arrive at a cracked runoff pipe jutting from a collapsed structure, leaking a slow, steady stream of dark, chemical-sheened fluid into the water. Around its base: a wide dead zone, bleached and lifeless. The creature stops well clear of it, unwilling to go closer.
* **Audience vs. Jack knowledge:**
  * Both: Fully resolved — this is the cause, and it's spreading
* **BOARD NOTE `[REQUIRED]`:** Hold on the pipe and the dead zone long enough for scale to register before cutting back to Jack's reaction.
* **BOARD NOTE `[SUGGESTED]`:** A single dead fish drifting past in the foreground as the reveal lands — small, specific, and more affecting than a wide shot of devastation alone.

### If this act isn't landing:
> If the reveal feels too didactic, **remove** any shot that lingers on the pipe as a "villain object" — keep the visual focus on the dead zone's *effects* rather than the machinery itself. The damage should feel more important than its source.

---

# ACT III — The Seal

### Tone & Palette
* **Visuals:** Harder-edged, physically tense. Not explosive (501). Not claustrophobic (502). Effortful. Precarious. Jack working against something bigger than a single fight.
* **Audio:** Grinding metal, rushing water, strained breathing. NO score. NO silence-drop for shock this time. Tension sustained, not detonated.

---

## Beat 5 — The Work
**Pacing:** `SUSTAINED TENSION`

* **What happens:** Jack attempts to seal or divert the leak using wreckage and debris from the surrounding ruins, racing against the pipe's continued output. The creature, still keeping its distance from the contaminated water, positions itself to block a secondary runoff channel **with its own body**, taking on real risk to buy him time.
* **Audience vs. Jack knowledge:**
  * Audience: Sees this as the emotional payoff of 501–502 — debt repaid in both directions now
* **BOARD NOTE `[REQUIRED]`:** At least one shot showing the creature visibly straining against the current it's blocking — this must read as **genuine sacrifice**, not passive presence.

---

## Beat 6 — The Seal Holds
**Pacing:** `CLIMAX (quiet version)`

* **What happens:** Jack wedges the last piece of wreckage into place. The flow stops, or slows to a trickle. Both of them are left breathing hard, some distance apart, in water that is finally, locally, **still**.
* **Audience vs. Jack knowledge:**
  * Resolved practically — immediate danger contained
  * Larger contamination beyond this one pipe left unaddressed, deliberately
* **BOARD NOTE `[REQUIRED]`:** Avoid triumphant framing — this is **relief**, not victory. Keep both figures low in the frame, exhausted rather than heroic.

### If this act isn't landing:
> If the cooperation reads as too easy or too neat, add **one moment of near-failure** mid-beat — the wreckage slipping once before Jack resets it — so the effort has visible cost rather than resolving on the first attempt.

---

# ACT IV — Resolution

### Tone & Palette
* **Visuals:** The sickly yellow tint recedes *only partially* — this ending should not fully clean the water, signaling the damage is real and lingering, not undone.
* **Audio:** Ambient life sound returns thin and tentative, not full — a deliberate contrast to `502`'s fuller resolution.

---

## Beat 7 — Departure, Changed

* **What happens:** The creature doesn't linger this time, and doesn't need to — something has settled between them that no longer requires the extended look from the previous episode. It submerges and moves on without ceremony. Jack watches the trickle from the sealed pipe a moment longer, then continues his crossing.
* **Audience vs. Jack knowledge:**
  * Audience left understanding this relationship has moved past *debt* into something closer to *mutual recognition* — while the episode is honest that the wider contamination problem is bigger than either of them solved today.

---

## PRODUCTION & DESIGN NOTES

* **Backgrounds:** Introduce the sickly yellow contamination tint as a new palette element layered over the established swamp look — should read as an *intrusion* on the previous two episodes' visual world, not a full repaint.
* **Props:** The runoff pipe and collapsed structure should carry the same **Aku-industry design signature** established in `502`'s trawler wreck, tying all three episodes to one slowly-revealed environmental throughline.
* **Sound:** Continue the trilogy's **no-vocalization rule** for the creature — its communication in this episode is entirely through positioning, pacing, and the repeated look-back motif.
* **Rough Silence Budget:** Lighter than `501`, heavier than `502`. One meaningful quiet stretch around the Act II reveal (`Beat 4`) — functioning as **somber**, not tense — distinct in emotional quality from both previous episodes' silences.
