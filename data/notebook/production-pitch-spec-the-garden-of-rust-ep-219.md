# Production Pitch Spec: "The Garden of Rust" (EP 219)

A production-ready outline engineered for visual-first storytelling: minimal dialogue, sensory/pacing-driven beats, silence-as-mechanic, "hold-stretch-explode" kinetic rhythm, and bloodless robotic violence.

---

# PRODUCTION OUTLINE: EPISODE 219

**TITLE (WORKING):** "The Garden of Rust"  
**WRITERS / TEAM:** Genndy Tartakovsky, Paul Rudish  
**TARGET PACING:** Slow Bloom / Patient Dread $\longrightarrow$ Sudden Fracture / Kinetic Burst $\longrightarrow$ Quiet Descent  
**KEY THEME:** Growth vs. Decay / Patience as a Weapon  

---

## ACT I: THE GREENHOUSE AT THE EDGE OF THE WORLD

### Tone & Palette
* **Visuals:** Saturated jade greens, hothouse gold light through fogged glass panels. Overgrown vines choke rusted machinery. Lineless, painterly, humid haze.
* **Audio:** No music. Buzzing insects (mechanical, subtly wrong — a faint servo whine under the buzz), dripping condensation, distant creak of settling metal.

### Beat 1: The Threshold (Pacing: Very Slow / Hold)
* Jack, dust-worn from desert travel, comes upon an immense derelict greenhouse dome rising out of a dead salt flat — impossibly out of place.
* **Board Note:** *Wide establishing shot, Jack a small dark figure against the dome's curved glass horizon. Hold 6 seconds before he moves.*
* He pushes through a rusted-open door. Inside: a jungle has swallowed the machinery whole.

### Beat 2: Something Tending the Garden (Pacing: Creeping Tension)
* Jack notices things are *too* orderly beneath the wildness — pruned branches, raked soil, a trimmed path.
* A soft *SNIP... SNIP...* echoes from deeper in the dome, rhythmic, unhurried.
* Jack follows the sound through hanging vines to a clearing where **THE GARDENER** kneels: a spindly, insectile robot with dozens of thin articulated limbs ending in shears, its "face" a cluster of dim, unblinking lenses. It is pruning a single black, thorned flower with total absorption.
* It does not look up.

---

## ACT II: THE PATIENCE TEST

### Tone & Palette
* **Visuals:** Color script shifts — deep emerald shadow pooling around shafts of warm light. Introduce horizontal tri-panel splits during the Gardener's movements (limb / shears / flower).
* **Audio:** The snipping continues underneath everything, a metronome that never stops, even during dialogue or combat.

### Beat 3: The Unanswered Bow (Pacing: Stillness / Unease)
* Jack bows, states his purpose is only passage. The Gardener says nothing, keeps pruning.
* **Dialogue (Sparse):**
  > **THE GARDENER:** *(voice like dry leaves rubbing together, barely above a whisper)*  
  > *"You are already growing wrong. I will fix that."*
* Jack steps forward to pass by. Every limb of the Gardener moves at once — not attacking, just *repositioning*, shears clicking shut around Jack's path on all sides without touching him. A cage of blades, motionless.

### Beat 4: The Slow Cut (Pacing: Sustained Micro-Tension)
* Jack tests an opening; a single shear-limb flicks out and clips a lock of his hair before retracting — instant, silent, precise.
* **Boarding Focus:** *Play this almost entirely in silhouette against the green light. No music. Let the SNIP be the only percussion in the scene.*
* Jack realizes: this isn't a brute-force enemy. It reacts only when he moves carelessly. Patience is the only way through.

---

## ACT III: STILLNESS AGAINST STILLNESS

### Tone & Palette
* **Visuals:** The dome's colors desaturate toward monochrome jade as tension peaks — like held breath.
* **Audio:** Strip away insect buzz and drips. Only the snipping remains, growing almost imperceptibly faster.

### Beat 5: The Match (Pacing: The Hold)
* Jack kneels across from the Gardener and mirrors its stillness exactly — matching its rhythm, breathing in time with the shears.
* Hold on both figures, motionless, for 7 full seconds. Only the flower between them moves, trembling slightly.
* The Gardener's snipping falters — thrown by an opponent who isn't fighting its rhythm, but joining it.

### Beat 6: The Break (Pacing: Hyper-Fast / On Ones)
* The instant the Gardener's rhythm breaks, Jack moves — a single fluid draw-and-strike, animation snapping to "on ones."
* Split into three vertical panels:
  * Left: Jack's katana clearing its sheath.
  * Center: the Gardener's dozens of limbs converging too late.
  * Right: the black flower, untouched, at the center of it all.

### Beat 7: The Harvest (Pacing: Climax, Then Silence)
* One clean vertical stroke. No blood — trimmed cable and dry, papery husk scatter like cut stems.
* The Gardener folds inward, limbs curling like a dying spider, and goes still.
* 4 seconds of nothing. A single leaf settles.
* **The FX Payoff:**
  1. Severed limbs clatter to the stone floor in a soft cascade (`tik-tik-tik-tik`).
  2. The lens-cluster face dims one lens at a time, not all at once.
  3. The black flower, finally free of its caretaker, slowly unfurls one more petal.

---

## ACT IV: RESOLUTION

### Tone & Palette
* **Visuals:** Shafts of late gold light now reach the clearing floor for the first time — the canopy has opened slightly where the Gardener fell.
* **Audio:** Insects resume, ordinary and unremarkable. No score, or the faintest single sustained string note.

### Beat 8: The Aftermath
* Jack studies the black flower a moment. He does not pick it.
* He gently resets one fallen shear-limb upright in the soil beside it, like a marker or a small kindness.
* He exits through the broken dome wall into open desert light, leaving the garden to grow — untended, but alive.

---

## PRODUCTION & DESIGN NOTES FOR DEPARTMENTS
* **Backgrounds:** Dense layered foliage silhouettes, glass dome should read as both cathedral and cage — tall vertical framing throughout Act I.
* **Props:** The black flower is the emotional anchor of the episode — keep its design simple and graphic so it reads instantly in silhouette against any background.
* **Sound:** The snip effect is the episode's heartbeat — record a real scissor/shear foley, layered dry with almost no reverb, so it always feels close and intimate even in wide shots.
