# THE GILDED VAULT
*Scene SC-01 — Draft v01*

---

### Logline
Two rival sphinx cats — bodybuilder-lean, hairless, and utterly serious about it — face off over a treasure vault neither can rightfully claim. What begins as a territorial standoff over gold becomes a genuinely even physical contest, settled not by who's strongest, but by who's willing to walk away first.

### Theme
Owning something and having earned it are not the same claim — and only one of those actually holds up under pressure.

## Setting

**THE GILDED VAULT, UNDER THE OLD EXCHANGE**

*A sunken stone vault beneath a collapsed merchant house, accessible only through a narrow drainage tunnel. Centuries of coin, gilded idols, and unclaimed treasure sit in loose, uneven piles across the floor — enough wealth that no single creature could carry a fraction of it, which is precisely the point of the dispute. Torchlight from wall sconces long since converted to some other flickering source throws hard shadows across engraved stone columns.*

*Both cats are lean, muscular, entirely hairless — built less like house pets and more like something that has been doing pull-ups on the vault's iron support beams for years. Neither blinks first easily.*

---

## Beat One — The Standoff

*RAMSES, broad-shouldered, scarred along one flank, sits atop the largest coin pile like it was built for him. He watches the tunnel entrance without moving.*

<p align="center"><b>RAMSES</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
You're standing in my vault.
</div>

*BASTET-KHU enters through the drainage tunnel, low and controlled, muscle visibly working under bare skin with each step.*

<p align="center"><b>BASTET-KHU</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
Funny. I don't remember your name on the deed.
</div>

<p align="center"><b>RAMSES</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
There isn't a deed. There's whoever's strong enough to sit on top of the pile.
</div>

---

## Beat Two — The Terms

*Bastet-khu circles the base of the coin pile, unhurried, assessing the terrain rather than the cat.*

<p align="center"><b>BASTET-KHU</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
<i>(almost bored)</i>
So it's not about who found it. It's about who can hold it.
</div>

<p align="center"><b>RAMSES</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
It's always been about who can hold it.
</div>

*Bastet-khu stops circling. Plants all four paws.*

<p align="center"><b>BASTET-KHU</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
Good. Then we agree on the terms.
</div>

---

## Beat Three — The Claim, Restated

*A long beat. Neither moves. This is the last moment either could still walk away without a fight.*

<p align="center"><b>RAMSES</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
<i>(quieter, almost a warning)</i>
I've held this vault three winters. Nobody's taken it off me yet.
</div>

<p align="center"><b>BASTET-KHU</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
Three winters isn't a claim. It's just a habit nobody's broken.
</div>

---

## Beat Four — The First Move

*Ramses descends from the coin pile, unhurried, muscle rolling visibly with every step — the first real physical commitment of the scene.*

<p align="center"><b>RAMSES</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
Then let's break it.
</div>

*Steel doesn't clear leather here — claws do, unsheathing with a sound like a drawer full of knives.*

<p align="center"><b>BASTET-KHU</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
Was hoping you'd say that.
</div>

## Fight Construction — Sub-Storyboard System

*Choreography below is built in locked beat-sequences rather than single panels. Dialogue occurs only in resolution beats and the gaps between sequences — never inside an active phase. Both combatants are matched in raw physical power throughout; superiority is decided by who runs out of sequences first, never by any single strike.*

```
   ground. Hold 2 frames on the lock. Reset, not

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### ⚔ SEQUENCE 3 — Traded Blows *(4-beat: A / B1 / B2 / C)*

```
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 A · ANTICIPATION

   weight low, muscle coiled on opposite sides of a
   shared pile of coin.

 B1 · IMPACT (LEFT)
   Split-screen, left panel: Ramses's strike
   connecting across Bastet-khu's flank. Hold 2
   frames.

 B2 · IMPACT (RIGHT)

   counter-strike connecting across Ramses's

 C · RESOLUTION
   Both stagger the same distance, same angle,
   scattering coin in identical arcs — visual
   confirmation of exactly matched power.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

*(No dialogue in this sequence's resolution — both are too winded, and the scattering coin does the talking.)*

### ⚔ SEQUENCE 4 — The Exhaustion Beat *(3-beat: A / B / C, degraded)*

```
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

 C · RESOLUTION (EXTENDED)
   Both break apart, sides heaving, claws still bared
   but neither advancing. Hold 3 seconds.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

<p align="center"><b>BASTET-KHU</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">

### ⚔ SEQUENCE 5 — The Decision, Not the Kill *(5-beat: A / B1 / B2 / B3 / C)*

```
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
 A · ANTICIPATION
   Ramses gathers everything left for one final
   committed lunge — full extension, claws bared.

 B1 · CONTACT BEGINS
   The lunge closes distance — no longer stoppable
   by either party's reflexes alone.

 B2 · THE CHOICE
   Bastet-khu doesn't dodge. Stance visibly shifts
   from defense to acceptance — this beat isolates
   the decision, separate from the contact that
   follows.

 B3 · IMPACT (NON-LETHAL)
   Claws land, but sheathed at the last instant —
   a shove rather than a strike. Hold 4 frames — long
   enough to register this was a choice, not a
   failed hit.

 C · RESOLUTION
   Ramses doesn't press the advantage. Claws retract
   on their own.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

<p align="center"><b>BASTET-KHU</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
<i>(breathing hard, settling back onto all fours)</i>
There it is. That's what you actually wanted to prove.
</div>

<p align="center"><b>RAMSES</b></p>
<div style="max-width: 380px; margin: 0 auto 28px auto; text-align: left;">
<i>(also breathing hard, a low, exhausted rumble that might be a laugh)</i>
Took me three winters to admit it.
</div>

*They stand there a moment, coin scattered between them, sides still heaving — not allies, not enemies, just two things built the same way, who finally found something built just as strong.*

## Production Notes

**Staging**  
The vault should feel genuinely vast, coin piles varying in scale so the fight has real terrain to use — unstable footing, sightline breaks, height differentials.

**Character Design**  
Both cats built lean and visibly muscular under bare skin — silhouette-first design discipline; each must be instantly distinguishable from the other in pure black silhouette (Ramses broader through the shoulders, Bastet-khu longer through the spine).

**Color & Coat**  
Ramses reads warmer — bronze-grey skin tone with darker ear tips and a visible old scar along one flank, used as a consistent silhouette landmark across every sequence. Bastet-khu reads cooler — slate-grey skin tone, unmarked, relying purely on leaner proportions rather than a scar or marking to stay legible against him in wide shots.

**Sound**  
Claw-on-stone and claw-on-claw as the primary Foley layer; coin scatter as a secondary texture marking impact zones; no score during Sequences 1–4, a single low sustained tone permitted only at Sequence 5's resolution.

**Beat-Count Logic**  
2-beat (Sequence 1), 3-beat (Sequences 2 and 4), 4-beat (Sequence 3), 5-beat (Sequence 5) — chosen by what each moment needs, matching the discipline established in this format's prior use.

**Dialogue Placement Discipline**  
Speech occurs only in resolution beats and gaps between sequences, never inside an active phase.

**Silhouette Compliance**  
Every impact beat must independently pass the blackout test before boarding proceeds.

**Physical Scale Reference**  
Both cats should read as larger and more muscular than a realistic sphinx cat's proportions would allow — visible definition through the shoulders and haunches even at rest. This exaggeration should be consistent and matched between them; neither should out-scale the other at any point, since the entire fight's tension depends on the audience never being sure who has the physical edge.

**Coin & Debris Behavior**  
Scattered coin should behave as a legible tell throughout the fight — denser scatter patterns mark harder impacts, and by Sequence 4 the vault floor should visibly show accumulated disruption from Sequences 1–3, so the environment itself tracks the fight's escalation without needing a dialogue line to state it.
