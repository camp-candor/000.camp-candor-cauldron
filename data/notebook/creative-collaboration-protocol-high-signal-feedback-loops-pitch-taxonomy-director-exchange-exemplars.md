# Creative Collaboration Protocol: High-Signal Feedback Loops, Pitch Taxonomy & Director Exchange Exemplars

An operational craft and collaboration specification establishing the mechanics of high-signal creative exchange with senior designers and directors, outlining the 50-item granular testing taxonomy, and formalizing the psychological shift from meta-tool isolation to interpersonal connection.

---

## 1. The Core Human Tension: Isolation vs. Connection

The instinct to build elaborate orchestration layers before writing a single line of narrative often masks a deeper vulnerability:
* **The Delegation Trap:** Asking *"how do I get another person to author the first scene?"* is another form of creative avoidance. Delegating the initial creative spark hands over the core voice before proving it can be sustained.
* **The First-Slice Mandate:** Write the first draft yourself—rough, fast, and unvarnished. Handing a collaborator a concrete, imperfect artifact invites a genuine reaction; handing them a complex specification invites passive confusion.
* **Infrastructure vs. Connection:** Slack bots, ingestion funnels, and relational databases provide *contact*, not *connection*. Creative connection arises exclusively when collaborators interact with a tangible, vulnerable piece of work.

## 2. Granular Testing Taxonomy: 50 High-Signal Micro-Prompts

A diagnostic catalog of minimal, finished narrative artifacts designed to test specific creative layers with collaborators without dumping entire manuals on them:

### A. Bog's Voice (Character Authenticity)
1. Bog counting non-food items out loud while speaking of them as scarce calories.
2. A single sentence where Bog almost expresses gratitude, cutting himself off mid-word.
3. Bog detailing past sacrifices without identifying what was sacrificed.
4. A mundane inventory list functioning as an unspoken ledger of grievances.
5. Bog countering a direct question with a grim statistical fact about scarcity.
6. Two lines of Bog addressing someone who is not in the room.
7. Bog observing another person receiving praise for labor he quietly performed.
8. A single threat masked as paternal generosity.

### B. Two-Line Friction Exchanges (Relational Dynamics)
9. Bog making a martyred statement; Spleen deflecting with weaponized casualness.
10. Spleen attempting individuation; Bog treating it as catastrophic betrayal.
11. Wart stating a naive, unvarnished truth that silences both Bog and Spleen.
12. Mum Grissel breaking hours of silence with a single declarative sentence.
13. Spleen retreating behind the Dented Thimble mid-argument.
14. Bog and Spleen approaching accidental consensus, inducing intense discomfort.
15. Wart asking an innocent question exposing Bog’s concealed failure.

### C. Atmospheric Geography (Environmental Pulse)
16. The Warm Pipe from the perspective of an entity denied bodily privacy.
17. The Pantry Wall described strictly through layers of stale aroma.
18. The Attic Stair described by how far an acoustic transient travels in cold air.
19. The Splinter Gap as a threshold perpetually midway through structural collapse.
20. A room described entirely by what is socially forbidden to utter within it.

### D. The Load-Bearing Possession (Material Culture)
21. The Unfinished Carving: articulating what remains deliberately uncarved.
22. The Dented Thimble: preserving worthless metal as an instrument of leverage.
23. The Three Matched Buttons: the exact transaction where they were surrendered.
24. A single sentence reflecting Bog counting possessions instead of kin.

### E. Mechanical Outcomes Humanized (Engine-to-Emotion Translation)
25. The visual signature of a *Costly Win* registered on an adversary's face.
26. The internal physical sensation of a mechanical *Backfire*.
27. A *Withdrawal*: an entity choosing silence mid-conflict and what that silence buys.
28. A *Mutual Wound* narrated exclusively from one participant's perspective.
29. The exact moment an actor realizes their social *String* holds zero leverage.

### F. Paranoia Clocks (Tension & Dread)
30. Mum Grissel’s silence perceived through a thin partition from an adjacent room.
31. The subtle, unacknowledged environmental signs of `human_spring_cleaning`.
32. A beat where the Splinter Gap groans and nobody acknowledges the sound.
33. The internal dread of living beneath a countdown devoid of numbers.

### G. Aesthetic & Sensory Taste-Checks (World Palette)
34. Describing the visual color palette without naming characters or narrative events.
35. Framing what the camera observes during a five-second hold with zero dialogue.
36. The acoustic soundscape of an empty, tension-filled room.
37. The specific color frequency belonging to Bog and its physical origin.
38. The olfactory signature of the production.

### H. The Gothic-Domestic Tone Test (Anti-Parody Safeguards)
39. A joke cracked by Spleen that fails to land, revealing underlying desperation.
40. The most pedestrian sentence Bog can utter that carries devastating subtext.
41. A single beat balancing between deadpan comedy and existential dread.

### I. Direct Diagnostic Reaction-Bait
42. *"Does Bog read as an authentic human or a stock archetype?"* (attaching one paragraph).
43. *"Which of these two lines sounds like him?"* (A/B testing a beat).
44. *"Where does this scene lose tension, if anywhere?"* (short scene evaluation).
45. *"What question do you need answered after reading this?"*

### J. Domain-Expert Collaborator Invitations
46. To a visual artist: providing bare mechanical constraints for the Pantry Wall and observing their design.
47. To a sound designer: asking what Mum Grissel's silence sounds like using bare environmental facts.
48. To someone who understands grief: testing whether `crisis_grissel_speaks` feels earned.
49. To someone with siblings: assessing whether the Bog/Spleen friction reads as authentic.
50. To an uninitiated reader: sharing the character roster alone and asking what story they anticipate.

## 3. Senior Designer Creative Brief Packet: The Creature Protocol

Senior collaborators reject adjective-heavy briefs and micromanaged shot lists. They require **clear boundary constraints with open design surfaces**:

### A. The Core Packet Components
1. **Mechanical & Lore Facts:** Bullet points detailing environmental role, danger tier, and associated clocks.
2. **Palette Lock:** Exact hex codes or swatch sheets; non-negotiable for continuity.
3. **Scale Reference:** Silhouette comparisons against known architectural elements (doors, rafters).
4. **Use-Case Definition:** Distinguishing hero assets from fleeting, 1-to-2-second background presences.
5. **Reference Showing Taste, Not Answers:** 3–5 annotated images illustrating what is close, what is wrong, and what is strictly banned.
6. **Open Questions:** Explicitly highlighting unresolved dilemmas to invite creative authorship.

### B. Exemplar: The Attic Stair Sentinel Brief
```markdown
[Main Channel Root Post]
Creature Brief: Attic Stair Sentinel (Working Title)
- Environment: Lives exclusively in the Attic Stair (high-danger, exposed, cold, sound carries).
- Clock Link: Tied to human_spring_cleaning; serves as an environmental herald.
- Role: Background fixture, not a talking cast member. Seen for 1–2 seconds max; reads as dread, not an action monster.
- Palette Lock: Cold, desaturated blue-grey chip set (Hex: #2B333E, #4A5568, #718096).
- Scale: Door-frame height; fits within rafters.

[Thread Reply 1]: [Image: Taxidermied bird mid-flight] -> Stillness implying motion; frozen pose logic.
[Thread Reply 2]: [Image: Rusted, tangled wind chimes] -> Material texture: assembled from discarded house materials.
[Thread Reply 3]: [Image: Fanged movie monster] -> BANNED DIRECTION. Too threat-forward; avoid combat creature tropes.
[Thread Reply 4]: [Image: Moth on wallpaper] -> Concealment: nearly invisible until observed directly.
[Thread Reply 5]: [Image: Jointed antique marionette] -> Silhouette reference for assembled, non-organic limbs.
[Thread Reply 6 - Open Questions]:
- Has it always lived here, or did the house's decay manifest it?
- Does it require a discernible front/face, or is complete facelessness scarier in a 1-second glimpse?
```

### C. Dissecting the Collaborative Back-and-Forth
* **Welcoming Pushback:** When the designer points out that jointed marionette limbs read as an action jump-scare in motion, the creator concedes immediately rather than defending the reference.
* **Answering the Narrative Pivot:** The designer asks: *"Do the characters know what they saw, or is it deniable?"* The creator's definitive answer (*"Deniable, always"*) dictates the visual direction: the creature becomes **textured peeling wallpaper and lath**, lacking eyes or clean anatomical lines.
* **Rough Scrappy Iteration:** The designer submits quick 2-minute rough sketches testing texture-as-spine rather than waiting to deliver polished renders.

## 4. Senior Director Packet: The Screenplay Protocol

High-level directors have reviewed countless bloated pitch decks. Engagement requires **extreme restraint, zero special pleading, and space to direct**:

### A. Packaging Rules for Director Review
1. **The Scene in Full:** Provide one complete scene. Do not send synopses, treatments, or full scripts "for context."
2. **One-Line Logline:** A single sentence orienting the central dramatic tension.
3. **Restrained Tonal Anchors:** Reference a maximum of two films (*"In conversation with X, not imitating"*).
4. **Single Atmospheric Image:** One frame capturing texture or lighting; zero shot lists, storyboards, or casting notes.
5. **A Singular, Honest Uncertainty:** State exactly where the draft feels vulnerable (*"I don't know if the silence earns its weight or goes slack"*).

### B. Exemplar: The Goblin Engine Pantry Wall Exchange
```markdown
[Director Outreach Packet]
THE GOBLIN ENGINE — One scene, seeking your read.
Logline: A father who has tracked every sacrifice made for his family is finally asked "for what," and has no answer that isn't a threat.
Tonal Reference: In conversation with Ordinary People, not imitating it.

INT. PANTRY WALL — NIGHT
BOG counts jars. Doesn't look up.
SPLEEN
You could just say thank you. Once. See how it feels.
BOG
I don't need it said back to me.
SPLEEN
Then why do you keep count.
Bog stops counting. Doesn't turn around.
BOG
So when the day comes somebody asks what I did around here — I've got an answer ready.
SPLEEN
Nobody's asking, Bog.
BOG
You just did.
Long silence. Spleen doesn't leave. Doesn't push either.

Note: Unsure if the trailing silence earns its weight or bails on the scene early.
```

### C. Dissecting the Director Feedback Loop
* **Structural Diagnosis Over Line Editing:** The director notes that the silence is being used as an escape hatch: *"You're treating the silence as an ending... I read it as where the real scene starts and you cut a page early."*
* **Demanding Physical Specificity:** The director asks what is physically in the jars. Grounding Bog's action in **recounting dried lentils** turns a generic prop into an emotional lever.
* **Under-Cutting Cleverness:** Spleen’s follow-up line is rewritten not as a sharp retort, but as an awkward, vulnerable step backward (*"I didn't mean it like a—I just wanted you to hear it back once"*), preserving the heavy domestic gothic register.
* **No Apologies:** Revisions are submitted cleanly without defensive framing or throat-clearing prefaces.
