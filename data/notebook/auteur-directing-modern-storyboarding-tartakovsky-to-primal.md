# Auteur Directing & Modern Storyboarding: Tartakovsky to Primal

A detailed breakdown of Genndy Tartakovsky’s directorial role as "conductor," the mechanics of action storyboarding, and the evolution of visual-first narrative into Adult Swim's *Primal* (2019).

---

## 1. The Director as Conductor: Tartakovsky’s Creative Authority

In a visual-first, board-driven pipeline, having elite background painters and designers is insufficient without a single unifying vision. Tartakovsky’s role operates as a musical conductor:

* **The Master of Time (The Animatic Metronome):** Tartakovsky dictates the exact frame duration of every shot in the edit bay. He decides whether the audience stares at an environmental painting for 10 full seconds of dead air, and calculates the precise millisecond when a sword strike shatters that silence.
* **Action Choreographer:** Choreographs combat with physical mass and momentum—specifying diagonal cuts, delayed slides of severed mechanical torsos, high-pressure hydraulic bursts, and deliberate pacing shifts between animating "on twos" (heavy wind-ups) and "on ones" (fluid, lightning strikes).
* **The Dialogue Filter:** Acts as the ultimate story editor. Strips out unnecessary dialogue pitched by board artists, replacing expository speech with micro-expressions in the eyes and subtle postural shifts.
* **Architect of Sound and Silence:** Fights for dead air in an industry terrified of silence. Orchestrates the contrast between harsh, synthetic rhythms in dystopian settings and delicate, organic ambient cues during contemplative character beats.

---

## 2. Storyboarding Mechanics: Energy Over Polish

* **Line of Action Over Detail:** Tartakovsky’s personal boards are notoriously loose and sketchy. Rather than clean lines, they prioritize kinetic energy—the sweeping curve of a spine during an impact, or extreme perspective on an incoming fist.
* **Camera & Sound Notation in Margins:** Detailed annotations dictate camera moves (*“FAST TRUCK IN,” “DUTCH ANGLE”*) and rhythmic beats (*“Hold for 3 beats… then SWISH-CLANG!”*).
* **Panel Volume for 22 Minutes:** 
  * Dialogue sitcoms typically require 250–300 storyboard panels.
  * Action-driven *Samurai Jack* required **350 to 500+ primary panels**.
  * Factoring in sub-frames (A/B/C micro-poses for wind-up, strike impact, and follow-through), an episode contains **over 1,000 individual sketches**, taking a boarding team 5–6 weeks.
* **Pipeline Artifacts & Format Evolution:**
  * *Seasons 1–4 (2001–2004):* Drawn in pencil on 3-panel standardized paper templates; pinned to corkboards for physical pitches; scanned into Avid/Final Cut Pro for Leica reel creation; exported to **DigiBeta tape** and shipped alongside paper copies to Rough Draft Studios in South Korea.
  * *Season 5 & Modern (2017–Present):* Drawn digitally in **Toon Boom Storyboard Pro** / Photoshop; pitched on digital monitors; exported directly as `.MP4` or `.MOV` animatics to cloud servers; final masters returned as **Apple ProRes 4444** or **MXF**.

## 3. The Evolution into *Primal* (2019): Weaponized Pure Cinema

*Primal* (Spear and Fang) represents the ultimate technical distillation of Tartakovsky’s visual-first methodology:

* **Zero Intelligible Dialogue:** Eliminates dialogue completely. Characters communicate exclusively through body language, roars, heavy breathing, and pupil dilation. The storyboard is the screenplay.
* **The Return of Heavy Ink (Frazetta & Moebius):** Abandons the lineless UPA style for thick, gritty black outlines inspired by 1970s heavy metal fantasy art (Frank Frazetta, Jean Giraud / Moebius). Scott Wills’s lush, textured background paintings starkly contrast with graphic, blood-stained character action.
* **TV-MA Visceral Weight:** Circumvents TV-Y7 censorship limits (which previously mandated robots bleeding oil). Graphic violence is treated as survival drama, characterized by 15-second ambient holds broken by sudden bone-shattering impacts.
* **Direct-to-Animatic Digital Pipeline:** Drawn directly into Storyboard Pro with built-in timing tracks, allowing real-time playback and pacing adjustments before handing off to Studio La Cachette in France.

## 4. Acoustic Storytelling: The Sound & Score of *Primal*

Led by sound designer **Joel Valentine** and composers **Tyler Bates & Joanne Higginbottom**, audio carries the entire narrative burden:

* **Vocal Performance as Narrative:**
  * *Spear (Aaron LaPlante):* Highly physical vocalizations modulating pitch and breath (e.g., sharp grunts for danger; sustained exhalations for grief).
  * *Fang the T-Rex:* Multi-layered composite vocal design. Aggressive roars blend elephants, tigers, and bears; affectionate or inquisitive rumbles combine crocodiles, pigs, and canine breathing.
* **Hyper-Real Visceral Foley:** Snapping celery for bones, wet fruit squishes for flesh tearing, and heavy leather slaps. Ambient insect and wind drop-outs signal approaching predators off-screen.
* **The Compositional Process:**
  * *No Temp Tracks:* Tartakovsky avoids existing musical reference tracks.
  * *Beat-Boxing Spotting Sessions:* During spotting sessions over silent animatics, Tartakovsky physically beat-boxes rhythms and sound cues, establishing the organic foundation for the score.
  * *Extreme Acoustic Restraint:* Higginbottom emphasizes not "over-filling the box." A single, finite sound tool communicates what the scene requires rather than ten competing instruments.
* **"Techno-Tribal" Instrumentation:**
  * Aggressive African folk-influenced percussion and driving primal drums.
  * Solo woodwinds, cellos, or somber brass for tragic, isolated emotional beats.
  * Distorted electric guitars and modular synthesizers to signal dark magic, unnatural cruelty, or advanced hostile civilizations.
  * Complete musical drop-outs during peak emotional grief, leaving raw silence or lone instruments.
