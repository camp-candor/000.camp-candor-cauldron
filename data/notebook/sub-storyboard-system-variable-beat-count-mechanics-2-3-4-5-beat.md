# Sub-Storyboard System: Variable Beat-Count Mechanics (2-, 3-, 4-, 5-Beat)

A structural choreographic and boarding methodology that breaks combat exchanges into discrete, locked sub-sequences with strict dialogue placement and silhouette compliance.

---

## 1. Core Principles of the Sub-Storyboard System

Traditional storyboard scripts treat fight scenes either as vague prose (*"They duel fiercely"*) or rigid 3-frame triads. The Variable Beat-Count system treats action as physical sentence structures scaled to the narrative weight of the moment:

* **Locked Sequences:** Choreography is designed as atomic, modular exchanges rather than isolated, floating panels.
* **The "Blackout Test" Requirement:** Every critical impact frame ($B$) must independently pass the silhouette blackout test—if filled with solid black, the spatial relationship (*weapon meets weapon*, *weapon meets body*) must be 100% legible through negative space alone.
* **Dialogue Placement Discipline:** Spoken dialogue is strictly quarantined to **Resolution ($C$) phases** and the interstitial pauses between exchanges. Dialogue is permanently forbidden from active anticipation or impact frames.
* **Matched Skill Architecture:** Superiority is determined by endurance, positioning, and psychological exhaustion rather than asymmetrical combat skill.

---

## 2. The Variable Beat-Count Matrix

The number of frames assigned to an exchange corresponds to its dramatic complexity:

```
[ 2-Beat: A ──> C ]               (Opening test / feeling distance / zero true impact)
[ 3-Beat: A ──> B ──> C ]         (Standard exchange: Anticipation ──> Impact ──> Reset)
[ 4-Beat: A ──> B1 / B2 ──> C ]   (Simultaneous split-screen dual impact)
[ 5-Beat: A ──> B1 ──> B2 ──> B3 ──> C ] (The Decisive Choice / Impact isolated from contact)
```

### 1. The 2-Beat Sequence (Anticipation $\longrightarrow$ Resolution)
* **Structure:** $A \longrightarrow C$ (No true $B$ impact).
* **Function:** Used for feeling out distance, low-commitment feints, and testing parries where blades lock without kinetic harm.
* **Example:** Traveler tests distance low; Gatekeeper locks blades at midpoint; both step back to identical reset positions.

### 2. The 3-Beat Sequence (Standard Triad: A $\longrightarrow$ B $\longrightarrow$ C)
* **Structure:** $A \longrightarrow B \longrightarrow C$.
* **Function:** Standard committed strike and follow-through.
* **Timing:** Hold 2 frames on $B$ (impact) for physical mass and kinetic weight.

### 3. The 4-Beat Sequence (Simultaneous Split-Screen: A $\longrightarrow$ B1 / B2 $\longrightarrow$ C)
* **Structure:** $A \longrightarrow B_1 \text{ (Left Panel)} + B_2 \text{ (Right Panel)} \longrightarrow C$.
* **Function:** Simultaneous counter-strikes. Splitting the impact into two parallel horizontal or vertical panels prevents one strike from reading as primary and the other as secondary, mathematically establishing identical combat skill.

### 4. The 5-Beat Sequence (The Narrative Choice: A $\longrightarrow$ B1 $\longrightarrow$ B2 $\longrightarrow$ B3 $\longrightarrow$ C)
* **Structure:** $A \longrightarrow B_1 \text{ (Contact Begins)} \longrightarrow B_2 \text{ (The Choice)} \longrightarrow B_3 \text{ (Impact)} \longrightarrow C$.
* **Function:** Reserved strictly for the climax or moral pivot of an encounter.
* **Mechanic:** Splits impact into three sub-frames:
  1. $B_1$: Blade begins unstoppable arc.
  2. $B_2$: Opponent visibly shifts posture from defense to acceptance.
  3. $B_3$: Non-lethal contact (e.g., flat of the blade landing on shoulder, held 4 frames).
* **Rule:** If used on a routine blow, a 5-beat sequence reads as bloated padding; used on a decisive turning point, it isolates conscious intent from reflex.
