### Metric Translation: Discomfort vs. Silence
* **Withheld Social Information:** Instead of withholding visual information (shadows, camera angles), the curve tracks *social hypocrisy*—what Larry knows about his own inconsistency before public confrontation shatters it.
* **The Two-Callback Rule:** Tracks the dual-thread braid (A-plot parking dispute, B-plot careless favor). An episode lacking at least two interlocking callback collisions (`Beats 4 & 6`) is structurally incomplete by the show's comedic standard.

---

### TARGET PACING SHAPE
`Minor social violation` $\longrightarrow$ `Escalation through principle` $\longrightarrow$ `Parallel B-story seeded` $\longrightarrow$ `Threads collide` $\longrightarrow$ `Anti-resolution`

### KEY THEME
> *Being technically correct is not the same as being right, and Larry will never understand the difference.*

### LOGLINE
> Larry takes a parking spot he insists he "called" with his blinker — a decision that spirals into a neighborhood dispute, drags in a favor he owes Jeff, and ends with Larry losing the moral high ground he never actually had.

---

## ACT I — The Spot

### Tone & Register
* **Visuals:** Flat, sitcom-naturalistic.
* **Audio:** Diegetic dialogue; pizzicato/bass theme sting only on scene cuts.

### Beat 1 — The Blinker Defense (Setup · Discomfort: 2/10)
* **What happens:** Larry waits for a spot with his blinker on. Another driver slips in. Larry confronts him on technical principle.
* **Audience vs. Larry knowledge:** Audience knows this is absurd; Larry is 100% self-righteous.
* **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`:** Must state "the blinker rule" aloud clearly; unscripted dialogue, but the premise is the load-bearing seed for later callbacks.
* **IMPROV ANCHOR `[SUGGESTED]`:** The other driver should be calm and reasonable to heighten Larry's unhinged pedantry.

---

## ACT II — Two Threads

### Beat 2 — Public Litigation (Escalation · Discomfort: 4/10)
* **What happens:** Larry recruits neighbors to validate his blinker rule, creating secondhand awkwardness.
* **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`:** At least one neighbor must show deep secondhand embarrassment *for* Larry.

### Beat 3 — The B-Story Seed (Parallel Setup · Discomfort: 5/10)
* **What happens:** Jeff asks Larry for a small family event favor. Larry agrees carelessly while distracted by his parking grudge.
* **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`:** Larry's distracted, careless agreement must be unmistakable to the audience.

---

## ACT III — Collision

### Beat 4 — The Blinker Rule, Weaponized (Sharp Turn · Discomfort: 7/10 · 🔁 CALLBACK)
* **What happens:** Larry's own rule is turned against him in an unrelated dispute.
* **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`:** Larry must be hoist by his own petard using Beat 1's exact logic.

### Beat 5 — The Favor Backfires (Attempted Fix · Discomfort: 6/10)
* **What happens:** Larry attempts to fulfill Jeff's favor using wrong assumptions, compounding the damage.

---

## ACT IV — The Collision Point

### Beat 6 — Everyone, At Once (Climax · Discomfort: 9/10 — PEAK · 🔁 CALLBACK)
* **What happens:** All aggrieved parties (neighbor, Jeff, family member) collide in a confined room. Larry is forced to defend both disasters simultaneously.
* **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`:** Larry gets no clean exit line; he must be denied the last word.

---

## ACT V — Anti-Resolution

### Beat 7 — Nothing Learned (Discomfort: 3/10 · 🔁 CALLBACK)
* **What happens:** Larry appears to concede, then immediately reasserts the blinker rule in a new scenario.
* **IMPROV ANCHOR `[REQUIRED]`:** Zero character growth. Confirming the anti-arc premise.

---

## Production Takeaway: Portability vs. Content
The structural skeleton (curve graphing, required constraint tiering, callback tracking) is fully portable across genres. However, while *Samurai Jack* locks **visual framing and duration**, *Curb Your Enthusiasm* locks **premise, social consequence, and callback debt**, leaving dialogue entirely unlocked for actor improvisation.
